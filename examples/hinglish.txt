# A buyer who writes Hinglish - romanised Hindi with English business words - which is how
# a large share of Indian B2B conversation actually happens in writing and on calls.
#
# Until this was handled the agent detected the switch and then answered in formal
# Devanagari, because `mixed` redirected to the Hindi phrase table. The buyer can read
# that; it is not a comprehension failure. It is a register failure, and switching someone
# into literary Hindi when they wrote Hinglish reads as correcting them.
#
# Note which words stay English in the replies - budget, website, catalogue, proposal.
# Those are the words the buyer used. Translating them would be more consistent and less
# like anything a person says.
#
# Run: pitchbot-talk --script examples/hinglish.txt
#
# Turn 1 is English, so the conversation opens in English.
We run an online toy store.

# Turn 2 is the first Hinglish turn. One turn is not evidence, so nothing changes yet.
Humein ek catalogue aur online payment chahiye.

# Turn 3 is the second, so the switch lands and is acknowledged in Hinglish.
Yeh hamare liye thoda mehanga lag raha hai.

# Turn 4 is a stall, in Hinglish. Stance detection had no romanised entries at all before
# this - safety worked in Hinglish and stance did not, so a Hinglish buyer could refuse
# contact but could not object to a price.
Main baad mein batata hoon.

# Turn 5 fills the budget.
Hamara budget 150000 rupaye hai.

# Turn 6 agrees, so the agent closes instead of asking another question.
Theek hai, shuru karte hain.
